Twice a year every employer must prove to SARS that the payroll tax it declared, the tax it paid and the tax certificates it issued all agree. That proof is the EMP501 reconciliation. When the three do not match, the result can be penalties, an "excessive liability" query, a lost ETI refund and, often, a red tax compliance status that blocks tenders.

## What the EMP501 must balance

SARS describes the reconciliation as matching the employees' tax deducted or withheld against the liabilities declared and the payments made. In practice, three totals must agree for the period:

| What | Where it comes from |
| --- | --- |
| Liabilities declared | Your monthly EMP201 returns (PAYE, SDL, UIF, less any ETI) |
| Payments made | What SARS has actually received and allocated |
| Tax certificates | The IRP5/IT3(a) certificates for every employee |

## The two reconciliation periods

| Reconciliation | Covers | When it is submitted |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Interim | 1 March to 31 August | SARS's guide gives a submission period of September to October |
| Annual | 1 March to the end of February | Dates are published each year by SARS |

The exact dates change each year. Check the current filing season dates on the SARS website. Interim certificates are not issued to employees; the annual certificates are.

## Why the totals drift apart

SARS's guide lists common causes of differences, including:

- payroll tax tables not updated in time;
- pay that fluctuates during the year;
- bonuses and tax adjustments when employees resign; and
- timing differences between the payroll and the EMP201.

Other common problems we see in practice are EMP201s declared but not paid in full, payments allocated to the wrong period, and employees missing from the certificate file.

## How to correct the differences

- **Before the EMP501 is submitted:** correct an EMP201 error with a request for correction on that EMP201.
- **After the EMP501 is submitted:** submit a revised reconciliation, and reissue any changed certificates to the employees concerned.
- **Never refund tax to employees through payroll:** SARS states that employers are not permitted to refund any employees' tax to employees under any circumstances.
- **During an audit:** revised reconciliations cannot be submitted while an audit is in progress.

If SARS finds a substantial difference, it may issue an **Excessive Liability Change** notice, and you must respond within the stated timeframe.

## What is at stake

- **Administrative penalties:** SARS can impose a penalty for late or missed reconciliations, including a recurring penalty for each month a complete EMP501 is not filed, subject to a cap.
- **Late-payment penalty:** a once-off 10% penalty on amounts paid late or not paid.
- **ETI refunds:** SARS says ETI refunds can only be claimed by submitting both the interim and annual reconciliations; otherwise the refund is deemed nil.
- **Employees' returns:** SARS uses the IRP5/IT3(a) data to prepopulate employees' ITR12 returns, so a wrong certificate becomes your employee's problem too.

Penalties can be disputed through a request for remission.

## Which channel to use

- **eFiling:** for reconciliations with up to 50 certificates.
- **e@syFile Employer:** for larger reconciliations; import certificates from the payroll and use the latest version.
- **SARS branch:** only up to five certificates.

Once you choose a channel for a reconciliation, SARS requires later transactions on that reconciliation to use the same channel.
