A compromise is an agreement in which SARS accepts payment of part of a tax debt in full settlement. It is not a write-off on request, and it is not available simply because a debt is large. But where a taxpayer genuinely cannot pay in full, it can be the outcome that best serves both the taxpayer and SARS.

## The legal basis

SARS lists compromise as one of the ways to deal with tax debt, under **sections 200 to 202 of the Tax Administration Act**. SARS explains that a senior SARS official may authorise compromising part of a debt where the purpose is to **secure the highest net return from the recovery of the tax debt**.

In plain terms: SARS asks whether it will recover more by accepting your offer now than by pursuing full collection, which may include attaching assets, liquidation or sequestration.

## When a compromise is worth considering

- The debt is correct and not in dispute (disputed debt follows the objection route instead).
- You cannot pay the debt in full now or in the foreseeable future, so a payment arrangement is not realistic.
- Full enforcement would recover less than you can offer.

## What SARS asks for

SARS's list of supporting documents for a compromise request includes:

| Document | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Annual financial statements no older than one year | Shows the financial position |
| Six months' bank statements | Shows actual cash flow |
| A 12-month cash-flow forecast | Shows what can realistically be paid |
| A list of assets and liabilities | Shows what SARS could recover through enforcement |
| A debtors' age analysis | Shows money owed to the business |
| A motivation letter with the offer amount and source of funds | Sets out the proposal |
| A Collection Information Statement (CIS) | SARS's standard financial disclosure |

The quality of these documents decides the application. Incomplete or inaccurate disclosure is one of the main reasons applications fail.

## The expedited compromise process

On 30 September 2025, SARS announced an **Expedited Tax Debt Process** under the debt compromise provisions, agreed with the Recognised Controlling Bodies (RCBs) that regulate tax practitioners. According to the release:

- applications opened through a dedicated single point of entry from 13 October 2025;
- SARS committed to resolving qualifying applications **within four weeks**;
- **non-disputed tax debts older than 12 months** qualify;
- entities in liquidation, estates, business rescue cases, deregistered companies, cases under criminal investigation or audit, and cases in the write-off process are **excluded**;
- applicants must provide comprehensive supporting documents, and inaccurate disclosure may mean the application is not considered;
- the normal compromise process remains open to all taxpayers.

The process started with RCBs, with SARS indicating it may later extend it, so check the current position with your RCB-registered tax practitioner before applying.

## What to expect

- SARS can decline the application.
- Taxpayers who remain non-compliant, or who are not in the compromise process, face normal enforcement, which SARS says includes writs of execution of court judgments.
