EMP501 reconciliation: fixing mismatches before SARS does

Why EMP501 reconciliations do not balance, how to correct EMP201s and IRP5s, the tax number rule and late penalties. Prepare before the annual window.

By MF Banda, SARS registered tax practitioner PR-0034776 · Published 20 November 2026

Every employer registered for PAYE must reconcile twice a year. The EMP501 brings together three sets of numbers: what you declared on your monthly EMP201 returns, what you actually paid to SARS, and what the employee tax certificates (IRP5/IT3(a)) say each employee earned and had deducted. When those three do not agree, the reconciliation will not balance, and the problem usually costs more to fix the longer it is left.

The interim window for 2026 closed on 31 October. The next deadline is the annual reconciliation, so now is a good time to find and fix mismatches.

What the EMP501 reconciles

SARS's guide describes the EMP201 as a monthly self-assessment return covering employees' tax (PAYE), the Skills Development Levy (SDL), Unemployment Insurance Fund (UIF) contributions and Employment Tax Incentive (ETI) amounts. The EMP501 reconciles the tax withheld from employees, the liability you declared and the payments you made to SARS, supported by the IRP5/IT3(a) certificates. These values should balance.

For the 2026 interim reconciliation SARS said the EMP501 must reflect:

  • PAYE, UIF and SDL values that match the EMP201 returns you already submitted;
  • the actual payments made during the period, excluding penalties and interest;
  • payroll information, employee income tax reference numbers and the IRP5/IT3(a) certificates for the period.

The two reconciliation periods

Reconciliation Period covered Submission window
Interim 1 March to 31 August 2026: 21 September to 31 October 2026 (closed)
Annual 1 March to the end of February Annual window listed by SARS as 1 April to 31 May; for the period 1 March 2026 to 28 February 2027, that is 1 April to 31 May 2027

SARS announces the exact dates each year, so check sars.gov.za before you plan your submission.

Where mismatches usually come from

In practice, an EMP501 fails to balance because one of the three sets of numbers has drifted from the others. Things to check:

  1. EMP201 versus payroll. Compare each month's EMP201 to that month's payroll report. A month that was declared incorrectly, or never declared, throws out the whole period.
  2. EMP201 versus payments. Each payment must be made against the right month's payment reference number (PRN). A payment allocated to the wrong month, or a short payment, shows up as a difference.
  3. Certificates versus EMP201. The total of the IRP5/IT3(a) certificates must agree with what was declared. Employees who left mid-year, late payroll corrections and manual payslips are common culprits.
  4. Employee details. Missing or invalid income tax reference numbers and ID numbers stop certificates from validating.

How to correct the numbers

Fix the EMP201 first. SARS's guide says an employer can correct errors through a request for correction (RFC) directly on the EMP201 return, and that it is not necessary to wait for the EMP501. Correcting the monthly return is usually better than forcing the difference into the reconciliation.

Know where the shortfall lands. SARS warns that an incorrect monthly PAYE calculation can attract penalties and interest, even when corrected on the EMP501, because any shortfall is attributed to the last month of the reconciliation period.

Correcting a reconciliation already submitted. SARS's PAYE page says a revised EMP501 is submitted with the relevant revised certificates and/or cancellations, with cancellations done on an EMP601. Use the same channel you used for the original: SARS's guide says that once a channel is chosen for a reconciliation, later transactions must use the same channel.

Corrections do not refund employees. SARS's guide states that corrections in the EMP501 cannot result in refunds of employees' tax to an employee, other than corrections made within the pay period.

Employee tax reference numbers

SARS says income tax reference numbers have been strictly enforced on e@syFile and eFiling since the February 2026 filing season, and that EMP501 submissions without the required tax reference numbers will not be accepted. Employees without a number can be registered through ITREG or BundleReg on eFiling or e@syFile, or at a SARS branch by appointment. Do this now, not in the last week of the window.

How to submit

Channel What SARS says
e@syFile Employer Available to all employers, regardless of the number of certificates; use the latest version
eFiling Up to 50 IRP5/IT3(a) certificates per submission
SARS branch Only for employers with 5 or fewer certificates, by appointment

SARS also notes that submitting does not necessarily mean completion. After you submit, check the submission status and your PAYE dashboard on eFiling for rejections or validation errors.

What it costs to get it wrong

  • Late submission: SARS states an administrative penalty equal to 1% of your annual PAYE, increasing by 1% for every month the return remains outstanding, up to a maximum of 10%.
  • ETI: ETI refunds can only be claimed by submitting the reconciliations, and SARS warns that employers who claim ETI can forfeit unused ETI if they do not submit.
  • Criminal liability: SARS says wilful or negligent failure to submit the EMP201 or EMP501 is an offence, punishable by a fine or imprisonment of up to two years.
  • Employees: SARS notes that late or incorrect reconciliations can lead to unexpected tax outcomes for employees when they file their own returns.

A short checklist before the annual window

  • Reconcile every month's EMP201 to payroll and to bank payments.
  • Submit RFCs for any monthly errors you find.
  • Confirm every employee has a valid income tax reference number and ID number.
  • Check certificates for leavers and late payroll changes.
  • Update e@syFile to the latest version and submit early, then check the PAYE dashboard.
