How to object to a SARS assessment (and why vague objections fail)

Disagree with a SARS assessment? The 80-business-day deadline, when to request reasons first, what a valid objection must contain and what happens next.

By MF Banda, SARS registered tax practitioner PR-0034776 · Published 20 November 2026

If SARS has issued an assessment or a decision you believe is wrong, you have the right to object. But an objection is a formal step with a strict deadline and minimum content. Objections that simply say "I disagree" are routinely disallowed, and an objection lodged too late may never be considered on its merits.

Check whether an objection is the right route

Not every problem needs an objection:

  • An error on your own return that you want to fix: SARS offers a Request for Correction for errors on an original assessment.
  • You do not understand why SARS adjusted your return: consider a Request for Reasons first (see below).
  • Late-payment interest: SARS states that you do not have the right to object to certain statutory interest, such as late payment interest on income tax, UIF and SDL.
  • Administrative penalties: these usually start with a request for remission; you can object if the remission request is refused or only partly allowed.

The deadline: 80 business days

SARS's rule is that an objection must be submitted within 80 business days after the date of the assessment or SARS decision. Business days exclude weekends and public holidays, so count carefully from the date on the notice, not from the date you opened it.

If you requested reasons, the 80 business days run from the date SARS notified you that adequate reasons were already provided, or from the date the reasons (or further reasons) were given.

When to request reasons first

A Request for Reasons asks SARS to explain the basis of an assessment raised after an original assessment, verification or audit. It must reach SARS within 30 business days of the assessment or decision. Once SARS confirms a valid request, the objection period is automatically extended.

Requesting reasons is useful when the assessment does not explain the adjustment well enough for you to answer it. You cannot object properly to a decision you do not understand.

What a valid objection must contain

According to SARS, the objection must:

  1. identify the specific part or amount of the assessment you dispute, including the source code or item description where applicable;
  2. explain why you believe that part is incorrect; and
  3. include supporting documents you have not previously given SARS.

SARS's own example: if travel expenses were disallowed because the logbook did not meet SARS requirements, the objection must include a logbook that does.

In practice, a strong objection reads like a short, organised argument: the item, the facts, the rule that applies, the calculation, and the evidence, in that order.

How to lodge it

For personal income tax, company income tax, PAYE, trusts and VAT, SARS has an automated dispute process on eFiling. You can also lodge at a SARS branch, with an appointment. Certain exceptions use the manual Notice of Objection form (ADR1), which cannot be submitted on eFiling.

What happens next

Step SARS's stated timeframe
SARS tells you the objection is invalid, with the grounds Aims to do so within 30 business days of receiving it
SARS asks for more documents Aims to do so within 30 business days
SARS decides the objection Aims to do so within 60 business days of delivery (45 business days after requested documents, where documents were requested)

If the objection is allowed in full or in part, SARS issues a revised assessment. If it is disallowed, the next step is an appeal.

Important: an objection does not stop collection

Lodging an objection does not by itself suspend payment of the disputed tax. If you cannot or should not pay while the dispute runs, a separate suspension of payment request is needed.
