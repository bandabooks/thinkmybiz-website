Missed the deadline to object? How late objections and condonation work

Missed the 80-business-day objection deadline? How SARS treats late objections, reasonable grounds vs exceptional circumstances, and the 3-year limit.

By MF Banda, SARS registered tax practitioner PR-0034776 · Published 20 November 2026

The normal deadline to object to a SARS assessment is 80 business days after the date of the assessment or decision. Missing it is common: the notice went to an old email address, the business was in crisis, or the taxpayer simply did not know. A late objection is not automatically lost, but the rules are strict, and the reasons for lateness matter as much as the merits.

The key rule: lateness first, merits second

SARS's guidance is that a taxpayer submitting a late objection must provide grounds (reasons) for the late submission. SARS will only consider the merits of the objection if a senior SARS official accepts those reasons.

So a late objection has two parts, and both must be strong:

  1. Why it is late, supported by facts and evidence.
  2. Why the assessment is wrong, as in any objection.

How much extra time SARS can allow

Situation What SARS can do
Reasonable grounds for the delay Extend the deadline by 30 business days
Exceptional circumstances Extend the deadline further, if a senior SARS official is satisfied they exist
More than 3 years after the assessment or decision No objection can or will be allowed

The three-year limit is absolute. If an assessment is approaching three years old, act immediately.

What counts as good reasons

SARS's page does not list them, and each case depends on its facts. In practice, a persuasive request explains:

  • when and how you actually became aware of the assessment;
  • what prevented you from objecting in time, with evidence (for example, medical records, proof of an incorrect address on SARS's system, or correspondence);
  • what you did once you became aware; and
  • why it would be unfair to refuse to consider a well-founded objection.

Vague reasons, such as "I was busy", are unlikely to succeed.

If SARS refuses to accept the late objection

If the lateness is not condoned, SARS will not consider the merits. However, SARS's guidance says you may object to that refusal, and if that objection is disallowed, appeal against the decision.

Two other points to remember

  • Payment continues: a late objection does not stop SARS collecting the debt. A separate suspension of payment request may be needed.
  • Prevention: keep your registered email, postal address and banking details current on eFiling, and check your SARS correspondence monthly, so that assessments do not go unnoticed.
