SARS penalty remission: when a request (RFR) can work

Charged a SARS penalty? What a request for remission must contain, the circumstances SARS accepts, why you must file first, and what to do if refused.

By MF Banda, SARS registered tax practitioner PR-0034776 · Published 20 November 2026

SARS imposes administrative penalties when returns are outstanding or payments are late. They add up quickly. The way to ask SARS to cancel or reduce a penalty is a Request for Remission (RFR). It is not an automatic waiver: it succeeds when the facts fit the circumstances SARS is allowed to accept and when the request is properly made.

How the penalties work

For a late income tax return, SARS charges a fixed-amount penalty under section 210 of the Tax Administration Act. According to SARS:

  • the penalty ranges from R250 to R16 000 a month, depending on taxable income;
  • it recurs each month the return remains outstanding, up to a maximum of 35 months; and
  • SARS advises submitting the outstanding return whether or not you agree with the penalty, to stop further penalties.

Penalties also apply to outstanding trust returns (ITR12T) for recent periods, and percentage-based penalties apply to late payments.

First step: remission, not objection

SARS states that to request remission of non-compliance penalties, administrative penalties or interest, you must first request remission and cannot directly object. An objection is only possible once the RFR has been submitted and considered.

What an RFR must contain

SARS requires the request, on the prescribed form or in writing, to include:

  1. a description of the circumstances that prevented you from complying;
  2. each period and amount you want remitted; and
  3. the transaction code from the penalty assessment letter.

When SARS can remit a penalty

Situation What SARS's rules allow
First incidence, or non-compliance lasting less than five business days (fixed-amount penalties) Remission of up to R2 000, where there were reasonable grounds and the non-compliance has been remedied
First incidence, or a penalty under R2 000 (percentage-based penalties) Remission where there were reasonable grounds and the outstanding capital has been paid
Exceptional circumstances Remission of the whole penalty or a portion

The exceptional circumstances SARS lists are:

  • a natural or human-made disaster;
  • civil disturbance or disruption in services;
  • serious illness or accident;
  • serious emotional or mental distress;
  • an act of SARS, such as an office error;
  • serious financial hardship; and
  • any other circumstance of similar seriousness.

The request must show, with evidence, how the circumstance actually prevented compliance at the time. A general statement that the business was busy or that the taxpayer forgot will not meet this standard.

Fix the non-compliance first

For several of the remission grounds, SARS requires the non-compliance to be remedied: the outstanding return submitted, or the outstanding tax paid. File first, then request remission. A request lodged while the return is still outstanding is unlikely to succeed, and the penalty keeps recurring.

How to submit

An RFR can be submitted on eFiling or at a SARS branch, with an appointment. SARS accepts manual requests only in exceptional circumstances, and PAYE disputes and remission requests must be made on eFiling.

If SARS refuses

If the RFR is disallowed or only partly allowed, you may object to the decision, and if the objection fails, appeal. The objection deadline is 80 business days after the decision. SARS's guidance notes that some transactions may be settled while the RFR, objection or appeal is handled.
