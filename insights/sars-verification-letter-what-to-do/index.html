SARS verification letter: what to send, how and by when

Selected for SARS verification? What verification is, how to submit documents, what happens to your refund, and what to do if you disagree with the result.

By MF Banda, SARS registered tax practitioner PR-0034776 · Published 20 November 2026

A verification letter is one of the most common letters SARS sends, and it is not an accusation. It means SARS wants to check what you declared. But how you respond decides whether the matter closes quietly or turns into a revised assessment, penalties or an audit.

What verification is

SARS describes verification as checking the information you gave in a declaration or return against third-party data, financial and accounting records and other supporting documents. Any taxpayer can be selected.

An audit is different: SARS examines your financial statements, accounting records and supporting documents more extensively, and SARS says an audit can take from 30 business days to 12 months. A verification can be referred for audit if SARS sees a financial risk.

Read the letter carefully

The letter states which documents SARS wants and the due date or timeframe for your response. Only send what supports the items in question, but send all of it, organised and labelled. Typical documents include IRP5/IT3(a) certificates, interest and investment certificates, medical and retirement annuity certificates, travel logbooks and records for business expenses.

Three ways to submit

Channel What to remember
eFiling Scan and upload the documents, then click Submit; the upload link closes once submitted
SARS Online Query System (SOQS) Include your verification letter, whose barcode links the documents to your records
SARS branch Book an appointment and take the letter with you

SARS's stated file rules: PDF, Word, Excel, JPG or GIF formats, a maximum of 5 MB per document and up to 20 documents. Plan your scans so that each file stays under the limit.

What happens after you submit

  • SARS says it will attempt to verify your information within 21 business days after you upload the requested documents.
  • If there is no issue, you receive a completion notice.
  • If SARS finds a discrepancy, it issues a revised assessment.
  • If SARS sees a financial risk, it may refer the matter for audit.

Refunds: if you have a refund due, SARS pays it only after verification is complete.

If you miss the deadline

If you do not respond, SARS sends a second letter. If you still do not respond by the deadline, SARS issues a revised assessment based on the information it holds, which often means deductions are disallowed. Penalties can follow. Respond on time, or contact SARS before the deadline if you genuinely need more time.

If you disagree with the outcome

You can dispute verification findings through the objection process. SARS also notes that you can approach its Voluntary Disclosure Unit at any time during verification or an audit if there is something you need to disclose.

Common mistakes

  1. Uploading documents but not clicking Submit on eFiling.
  2. Sending unrelated or illegible documents, which delays the outcome.
  3. A travel logbook that does not meet SARS requirements.
  4. Ignoring the letter because a refund was not expected, and then receiving a revised assessment.
