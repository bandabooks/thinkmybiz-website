Travel logbook requirements SARS accepts (and why claims fail)

Claiming business travel against a travel allowance? What your logbook must show, the odometer readings SARS expects, and the mistakes that get claims reduced.

By MF Banda, SARS registered tax practitioner PR-0034776 · Published 23 November 2026

If your employer pays you a travel allowance, part of it is taxed through payroll. When you file your ITR12 you can claim a deduction for the business use of your vehicle, and that claim can reduce your tax or produce a refund. SARS is clear on one point: without a logbook, you cannot claim the cost of business travel against your travel allowance.

SARS may ask you to submit the logbook to support your claim. A logbook that meets SARS's minimum requirements is your evidence.

Who can claim

You can claim a travel deduction if you received a travel allowance from an employer or principal and used a private vehicle for business. The claim is made on assessment, in your annual income tax return.

Travel between home and your normal place of work is private. It cannot be claimed, however far it is.

What the logbook must contain

According to SARS, for every business trip you must record at least:

What to record Example
Date of travel 14 May 2026
Kilometres travelled 86 km
Where you travelled Office in Midrand to client in Centurion and back
Reason for the trip Monthly site meeting with client

You do not need to record private trips, but every business trip must be listed. If you used more than one vehicle for business during the year, keep a separate logbook for each vehicle.

Odometer readings: the step people forget

SARS works out your business-use percentage from the year's total kilometres. You therefore need:

  • the opening odometer reading on 1 March, the first day of the tax year; and
  • the closing reading on the last day of February of the following year.

Total kilometres equals closing minus opening. Business kilometres are the total of your logged business trips. A missing 1 March reading is hard to reconstruct later, so take a dated photo of the odometer on that day.

Deemed cost or actual cost

There are two ways to calculate the deduction:

  • Deemed (cost scale) method. SARS publishes a cost scale table for each tax year in the introduction to its eLogbook, based on the vehicle's value. You still need the logbook to show the business kilometres.
  • Actual cost method. You claim the business share of what you actually spent, such as fuel, oil, repairs and maintenance, licence, insurance, wear and tear, and finance or lease charges. You need the logbook and records of every cost.

The actual-cost method can give a better result for some people, but it fails quickly if receipts are missing. Choose the method you can prove.

Paper, spreadsheet or app?

SARS accepts electronic logbooks, and it publishes a free SARS eLogbook for each tax year. The current one covers 1 March 2026 to 28 February 2027. Spreadsheets and tracking apps are fine, as long as they show the minimum information above and can be produced if SARS asks.

Keep it for five years

SARS may ask for your logbook to support your claim, so keep it for at least five years from the date you submit your return.

Why travel claims are reduced

Common problems that weaken a travel claim:

  1. A logbook "rebuilt" at year-end from memory or a calendar, with round numbers and repeated trips.
  2. Home-to-office travel logged as business.
  3. No opening or closing odometer reading, so the business percentage cannot be supported.
  4. Business kilometres that are implausible compared with the vehicle's total kilometres.
  5. Actual-cost claims without receipts, or costs for a vehicle not in your name or use.

If SARS has already asked for your logbook, send what was asked for, in a clear format, before the due date in the letter. Our guide to SARS verification letters explains the process.
