When must a business register for VAT? The new R2.3 million threshold

SARS raised the compulsory VAT threshold to R2.3 million from 1 April 2026. When you must register, when it is optional, and what deregistering involves.

By MF Banda, SARS registered tax practitioner PR-0034776 · Published 23 November 2026

For sixteen years, a business had to register for VAT once its taxable supplies passed R1 million a year. That changed in the 2026 Budget. According to SARS, from 1 April 2026 the compulsory VAT registration threshold increased from R1 million to R2.3 million, the first adjustment since 2009.

The voluntary threshold also moved, from R50 000 to R120 000.

The rules in one table

Your taxable supplies (12 months) What applies from 1 April 2026
Up to R120 000 You cannot register voluntarily. If already registered, SARS may notify you that it intends to cancel your registration.
More than R120 000, up to R2.3 million Registration is optional (voluntary).
More than R2.3 million Registration is compulsory.

"Taxable supplies" means sales of goods and services that attract VAT, including zero-rated supplies. Exempt supplies, such as residential rent, do not count towards the threshold.

How the 12 months are measured

The threshold is not tied to your financial year. SARS looks at any 12-month period, and also at what you are likely to supply in the next 12 months. SARS's own example: if your taxable supplies are more than about R191 667 a month and you expect no change, you will probably exceed R2.3 million and must register.

This matters for growing businesses. A large new contract can make registration compulsory before the year's total actually reaches R2.3 million. Watch your monthly sales and register once you will clearly exceed the threshold.

Should you register voluntarily?

Below R2.3 million it is your choice. Points to weigh:

  • Your customers. VAT-registered business customers can claim the VAT you charge, so it costs them nothing. Private individuals cannot, so your prices effectively rise by 15%.
  • Your costs. If you buy a lot of VAT-inclusive equipment and stock, registration lets you claim input tax.
  • Tenders and large clients. Some prefer or require VAT vendors.
  • Admin. You must issue valid tax invoices, keep records and file VAT201 returns on time, even for periods with no activity.

SARS also points out that turnover tax, an alternative simplified tax for micro businesses, is now available up to R2.3 million turnover. Its first R600 000 is taxed at 0%.

Already registered and now below R2.3 million?

If your taxable supplies are between R120 000 and R2.3 million, you may apply to cancel your VAT registration, but think it through first:

  • Cancellation is not automatic. You apply on form VAT123e with your reasons, and you keep charging VAT and filing VAT201 returns until SARS confirms your final tax period.
  • On cancellation you must pay exit VAT on business assets and stock you still hold where input tax was claimed. It is valued at the lower of cost or market value. SARS says this can be paid in six equal monthly instalments.
  • If your sales are likely to exceed R2.3 million again soon, deregistering may only create a second registration later.

If you should have registered under the old R1 million rule

The new threshold applies from 1 April 2026. If your supplies passed R1 million before then and you did not register, SARS notes that your registration can be backdated, with penalties and interest. SARS recommends the Voluntary Disclosure Programme in this situation. Get advice before SARS contacts you.
